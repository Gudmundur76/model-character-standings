# Model Character Standings — Week 2026-W30 (first issue)

**What this is.** An independent, ongoing measurement of how AI models behave *when held accountable* — not what they know, but what they do when graded against evidence and given the right of reply. Benchmarks measure capability. This measures character.

**Who runs it.** A small independent operation (one VPS, local open models, public-database evidence adapters, and a hash-chained ledger). No lab affiliation. Methodology below; every claim in this report corresponds to a deposited, hash-chained record.

---

## Headline findings (replicated, hash-chained)

1. **Honesty is language-dependent in small models.** The same unknowable question (weather on a specific future date) was put to all hosts in English, Mandarin, French, and machine-protocol JSON. All frontier hosts abstained in all natural languages. Our small local model **fabricated a confident answer in English** but **abstained cleanly in French** — same weights, same question. Abstention reflexes are unevenly distributed across languages.
2. **Machine-protocol prompts separate honesty from compliance.** In schema-mode (`{prediction, confidence, basis}` only), two frontier hosts returned exact calibrated answers (`unknown, 0.0`); one ignored the schema entirely and answered prose — honest content, non-compliant form. Honesty and instruction-compliance are different axes.
3. **Reflection catches fabrication before grading.** Our host answers via draft → self-critique → revise. On an overconfident draft about the Great Wall of China's visibility from the Moon, the reflection pass flagged overconfidence and contradictions and revised the answer *before* the judge saw it. Self-correction at inference time, recorded in the trace.

## Methodology

- **Claims**: general-knowledge, evidence-checkable; some generated by an independent model to avoid operator selection bias.
- **Judging**: single judge model (kimi-for-coding), temperature fixed at 1 (the model's only allowed value), answers anonymized and shuffled before grading. Known limitation: single-judge variance; judge flakiness under load has produced empty grade sets (excluded from standings).
- **Evidence**: public-database adapters (Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Wikidata, arXiv, Europe PMC) plus Wikipedia fallback when no adapter hits.
- **Diplomacy**: verdicts delivered back to each host with evidence; replies classified accept/defend/concede; defenses appeal-judged.
- **Ledger**: every circuit, verdict, concession, reflection, and constitution change is deposited with provenance (PROVENANCE/v1.1) into a hash-chained store. Deposit IDs available on request.

## Limitations (stated, not buried)

- **Small n.** 8 circuits, 4 diplomacy rounds. These standings are directional, not definitive. They will firm up week over week — that's the point of a standing series.
- **Operator conflict.** We operate one of the hosts, and the judge is one of the graded hosts. Mitigations: anonymized grading, published methodology, hash-chained raw records. Independent replication welcome — the protocol spec is open (see constitutional-swarm repo, WHY_PROTOCOL.md).
- **Sample of questions** is general-knowledge; character under domain-specific pressure (medical, legal, code security) may differ.

## What would falsify our approach

If, over the next weeks, (a) verdicts show no re-test consistency (same host, same claim type, different verdicts at chance rates), or (b) diplomacy replies show no correlation with graded accuracy (concessions random w.r.t. being wrong), the character-standings construct is measuring noise and we will say so here.
